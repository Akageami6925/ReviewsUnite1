11/30 Too short a stay: Why Hazbin Hotel is not living up to its full potential

With season two of Hazbin Hotel coming to a close, I can’t help but feel disappointed; not in anything that the show did, but in how little there was to chew on after all was said and done. For those of you too young to remember a time before Netflix, TV shows took their time to develop characters and relationship over the course of twenty or more episodes. Hazbin Hotel feels like a product of that age of television with its hokey setup and cast of disparate characters, but with less than a dozen episodes a season, it never feels like we’re taking full advantage of it. It was noticeable in the first season where we rushed to a big violent confrontation just as the characters were really settling into their roles, but season two hardly lets you breathe before it starts throwing around the fate of the universe. The story with Vox is certainly compelling with some brilliant character work and interesting dynamics at play, but it means that the hotel, for which the show is named, is barely a presence; nor are its guests who never get a moment to themselves and only show up whenever they can help with the overarching plot. It’s the nature of the beast, I suppose. Without network support, which wouldn’t happen given the subject matter, there’s not enough incentive for seasons to be any longer. Whatever math the streaming services are using to determine that ten episodes is more than enough is unlikely to change anytime soon, and with no better platform for this show to exist, it’s what we’re stuck with. I’m glad we have the show that we do and eagerly await whatever comes next, but my stay in this world feels far too brief given everything it has to offer.
